# Artwork specification

All game artwork is generated by Python scripts (Pillow, supersampled vector drawing, Lanczos-reduced)
and written to `Diamond.Core/Art/`, where it is embedded and loaded by name at run time.

- `tools/make_backgrounds.py` — the first-person scenes (`bg_*.jpg`) and full-screen pictures.
- `tools/make_sprites.py` — objects, creatures and features (`*.png`, RGBA).

Both scripts are deterministic (fixed random seeds) and run from the repository root:
`python3 tools/make_backgrounds.py`, `python3 tools/make_sprites.py`.

## Style

A painterly, high-resolution reinterpretation of an Oric adventure (Loriciels, 1984): bold, saturated
tropical colours, clean shapes with soft shading, gradients and texture noise, no outlines thicker
than needed, no text in any image. Daylight outdoors (turquoise sea, deep green jungle, black volcanic
rock, golden sand); underground is lit by a torch (warm orange light falling off to near black at the
edges; vignette). Consistent light from the upper left outdoors.

## Coordinates

The game's picture window is **240 x 136 virtual pixels** (the top of the Oric's 240 x 224 screen;
the text window is below it). Backgrounds are drawn at **6x: 1440 x 816**, saved as JPEG quality 90.

- Outdoor ground views: horizon at about y = 60..66 (virtual), ground from there to the bottom.
  Objects stand on the ground with their base between y = 112 and 132.
- Rooms and tunnels: back wall / floor line around y = 92..100; floor down to 136.
- The centre of the picture (x 80..160) is where the way ahead is (path, doorway, tunnel); keep it
  readable.

Sprites are RGBA PNG at **8 px per virtual pixel**, tightly cropped (transparent margin of at most a
few pixels), with the bottom edge of the image at the point where the thing touches the ground (or
the wall for wall-mounted things). The game scales each sprite to the virtual height listed below
(width follows the image's aspect ratio) and positions it by its bottom centre. Give sprites a soft
contact shadow only if it is inside the cropped image and looks right on both sand and stone.
Underground the game tints sprites warm/dark itself, so draw them in neutral light.
